## 2. Data and protocol

| dataset | role | split used |
|---|---|---|
| GTSRB | SignNet train / test | 39,209 train images split by **track** (≈30 frames of one physical sign) into 35,219 train / 3,990 val; official 12,630 test scored once |
| GTSDB (Roboflow export) | detector; end-to-end test | 383 / 108 / 54 scenes, 1360×800; never used to train SignNet |
| RSCD | surface model | 34,585 images (≤1,200 train per class + all mirrored val/test), **re-split by capture day**: 60 / 9 / 15 days → 25,264 / 3,737 / 5,584 images |

**Leakage in the official RSCD split.** RSCD file names start with the capture timestamp.
84% of official test images share the exact capture *second* with an official training
image, and 98% share the minute: the split is frame-level random over video. The same
EfficientNet recipe scores 82.2% (27-way) / 93.3% (friction)
on the official split against 64.7% / 85.9% on unseen days. The gap is
near-duplicate recall, so every headline surface number in this report uses the day split.

## 3. Traffic-sign recognition (SignNet)

SignNet: spatial transformer → SE-ResNet (3 stages, widths 64/128/256, 2.9M parameters),
48×48 input (sign box + 15% margin). AdamW, cosine schedule, label smoothing 0.1, weight
EMA, 40 epochs (16 min on a laptop RTX 3060). Augmentation runs batched on the GPU:
semantic flips (symmetric signs keep their label, mirror pairs such as turn left/right
swap it, speed limits never flip), affine, colour/gamma, blur, noise and random erasing.

| variant | test accuracy | macro-F1 | errors /12,630 | accuracy under corruption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SignNet (full) | 99.84% ± 0.02 | 0.9978 | 20 | 91.1% |
| - STN | 99.79% | 0.9971 | 27 | 92.5% |
| - SE attention | 99.82% | 0.9976 | 23 | 91.0% |
| basic aug only | 99.67% | 0.9952 | 42 | 86.2% |
| no augmentation | 99.34% | 0.9925 | 83 | 86.9% |

Accuracy under corruption = mean over 9 corruptions × 3 severities.

**Reading the ablation honestly.**
* Augmentation is the main driver: it accounts for most of the clean-accuracy gap and about
  5 points of corrupted accuracy (occlusion in particular).
* SE attention adds nothing measurable here.
* The STN adds about +0.05 points on clean data but *lowers* corrupted accuracy. It was kept
  for the clean result; on degraded input the no-STN model is the more robust choice.

Per-group accuracy: danger 99.71%, derestriction 99.72%, mandatory 99.77%, priority 99.88%, prohibitory 100.00%, speed limit 99.83%. Most frequent confusions: Speed limit (60km/h) → Speed limit (80km/h) ×5; Bumpy road → Road work ×5; Yield → Keep left ×2; General caution → Wild animals crossing ×2; Keep right → Turn left ahead ×2.

**Calibration.** Label smoothing leaves raw confidences squeezed (ECE 9.24%).
A temperature of 0.177, fitted on validation, brings ECE to 0.17%. The
abstain threshold (validation error ≤ 0.1%) is 0.603. On the clean test set it accepts
99.95% of signs and catches 1 of the 22 errors: the remaining mistakes
are *confident* ones (60 vs 80 km/h, near-black crops), which abstention cannot catch.

## 4. Sign detection and the scene pipeline

Faster R-CNN ResNet-50-FPN v2 (COCO weights), class-agnostic, anchors shifted one octave
down (16–256 px) for 16–128 px signs, multi-scale training 704–896 px, 30 epochs (57 min).

| | val (108 scenes) | test (54 scenes, 82 signs) |
|---|---|---|
| AP@0.5 | 98.0% | 95.7% |
| AP@[.5:.95] | 84.1% | 83.5% |
| 43-class AP@0.5 (detector × SignNet) | n/a | 94.7% |

At the validation-chosen threshold (0.86): recall 95.1%, SignNet names
98.7% of found signs correctly (cross-dataset: SignNet never saw GTSDB), so
**93.9% of all test signs are found and named**, at 0.04 false positives per frame.
Median latency is 121 ms per 1360×800 frame on the RTX 3060 laptop GPU. The test split is
small (one sign ≈ 1.2 points).

## 5. Road-surface condition

Three factorised heads (friction 6, material 4, unevenness 3) with masked losses: snow and ice
hide the material, and gravel or mud has no damage grade. Inverse-sqrt class weights. Input
128×192, or 192×288 (0.8× native RSCD) for the high-resolution runs (batch 64: at batch 128 the
192×288 runs exceed 6 GB of VRAM and page to host memory).

| model (day-disjoint test, n=5584) | 27-class acc | 27-class macro-F1 | friction | material | unevenness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SE-ResNet from scratch, multi-head (128x192) | 54.2% | 0.451 | 80.1% | 72.5% | 64.8% |
| EfficientNet-B0 flat 27-way (128x192) | 63.8% | 0.554 | 85.8% | 79.4% | 75.7% |
| EfficientNet-B0 multi-head (128x192) | 64.7% | 0.554 | 85.9% | 80.0% | 75.9% |
| EfficientNet-B0 multi-head (192x288) | 65.3% | 0.566 | 87.4% | 81.6% | 75.3% |
| ConvNeXt-Tiny multi-head (192x288) | 66.6% | 0.565 | 87.0% | 82.6% | 77.9% |

* **ImageNet pretraining is worth about 10 points** over the same task trained from scratch.
* **Resolution vs backbone.** Going from 128×192 to 192×288 with the same EfficientNet raises
  friction and material (texture detail) but not damage grading. Swapping in ConvNeXt-Tiny
  at 192×288 is what moves unevenness (+2.7 points) and the 27-class score.
* **Factorised vs flat heads tie on accuracy.** The factorised head is kept for its
  structure, not for a score gain; the release uses the model with the best *validation*
  macro-F1 (convnext_multi_192).
* Hardest classes (F1): water gravel (0.22), water concrete slight (0.25), wet asphalt smooth (0.28), water asphalt severe (0.32), wet concrete severe (0.44). These mostly sit on the wet ↔ water boundary and the
  slight ↔ severe damage boundary, which are graded continua.
* The temperature on the joint distribution is 1.11, which takes ECE from 7.9% to 4.3%.

### Out-of-domain gate

RSCD is filmed by a camera aimed at the road a few metres ahead, so a forward dashcam is
outside its domain. Without a guard, the model labels 69–88% of GTSDB road crops "standing
water" whatever crop is used, which would wrongly cut the advised speed. A Mahalanobis gate
(class means and shrinkage covariance) on per-channel mean/std of backbone blocks 1–4 was
added. With the threshold at the 99.9th percentile of RSCD validation it accepts
98.6% of unseen-day RSCD test images and rejects 98.1% of dashcam road crops.
Final-layer features rejected only 33–43%. (The layer set was chosen while looking at the
same GTSDB crops, so treat the rejection rate as indicative.) Rejected readings are shown,
labelled, and never change the advisory. The 99th percentile was too tight: unseen capture
days drift, and it rejected up to 20% of some real RSCD test classes. A rejected ice, snow
or water patch silently drops a hazard warning, while a dashcam frame that slips through only
errs towards a lower speed, so the looser threshold is the safer one.

### Road damage from the dashcam view

Because the surface model abstains on forward views, a second detector reads damage
directly from the dashcam frame: Faster R-CNN R50-FPN v2 (COCO-pretrained, standard anchor
pyramid since damage boxes are large and elongated) fine-tuned on RDD2022 for the four
classes every country labels: longitudinal, transverse and alligator cracks, and potholes.
35,984 labelled frames from Japan, India, Czech Republic, Norway, the US and a Chinese
motorbike camera (the top-down China drone set is excluded) are split 80/10/10 per
country. Only RDD2022's train folders carry labels, so the split is our own. Its file
numbers are shuffled, so a 256-bit difference-hash check was used instead of a capture-order
split: 5 of 3,597 test frames have a near-identical training frame.
Six epochs (5.2 h on the laptop GPU).

| class | test AP@0.5 | boxes |
|---|---|---|
| longitudinal crack | 0.587 | 2386 |
| transverse crack | 0.565 | 1035 |
| alligator crack | 0.661 | 989 |
| pothole | 0.502 | 611 |

At the score threshold that maximises F1 on validation (0.74), test precision is
62.2% and recall 55.8% (F1 0.588, the CRDDC'2022 metric;
mAP@[.5:.95] 0.272). By country, F1 ranges from 0.45 to 0.79:
Japan 0.59, India 0.45, Czech 0.46, Norway 0.45, United States 0.69, China MotorBike 0.79.
Validation F1 was still rising at the last epoch, so a longer schedule would help.
On the 54 German GTSDB test frames (a country it never saw, mostly well-kept roads) it
flags damage on 4 frames and never reports a pothole.
Single cracks are reported only; alligator cracking (×0.9) and potholes (×0.75, hazard
"high") lower the advised speed.

## 6. Advisory layer

`v_safe = v_limit · sqrt(μ / μ_dry)` keeps the dry-road braking distance of the posted
limit, where μ = E[μ | friction probabilities] with typical tyre-road coefficients (dry 0.75, wet
0.50, water 0.35, melted snow 0.30, fresh snow 0.22, ice 0.10). Damage scales the speed
further (slight ×0.9, severe ×0.75). Hazard level comes from μ, damage and warning signs.
These coefficients are an engineering prior, not a measurement.

## 7. Limitations

* German signs only; RSCD is Chinese roads with a road-facing camera. Scene-mode surface
  readings are mostly rejected by the gate, so a road-facing camera or road-patch mode is
  needed for surface assessment.
* The GTSDB test split is small, and the OOD rejection rate is indicative (see §5).
* The ConvNeXt surface model is 7× larger than EfficientNet-B0 (27.8M vs 4.0M parameters).
  It is still real-time for one road patch per frame, but it is the heaviest part of the pipeline.
* The damage detector misses faint cracks (recall about 56%) and is weakest on
  potholes (AP@0.5 0.50), the class that changes the advice most.
* Damage grading (smooth / slight / severe) remains the weakest head, at about 78%; the
  grades are a continuum, and RSCD labels are per-clip.
